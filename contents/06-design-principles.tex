\subsection{Coding Procedure and Expert Interview Process} \label{ssec:coding-procedure}

The design principles presented in this chapter are derived through a grounded theory approach following the methodology of \textcite{Gioia2013} and \textcite{Gioia2021}, complemented by the coding techniques of \textcite{Saldana2015}. The Gioia methodology structures the analysis as a progressive abstraction from first-order concepts, which remain close to the language of participants, through second-order themes to aggregate theoretical dimensions \parencite{Gioia2013,Gioia2021}. In this thesis, the pattern codes resulting from the second coding cycle described below serve as the first-order concepts of this structure, which are subsequently consolidated into second-order themes and aggregate dimensions.

Prior to coding, the raw transcripts of the study were pre-processed and annotated with additional metadata to support the coding process; the specific pre-processing and annotation steps are described in Section~\ref{ssec:coding-results}.

Coding was conducted in accordance with \textcite{Saldana2015}. During the first cycle, process coding was applied, as the object of analysis, the interaction between participants and the \ac{AI} system, can be decomposed into a sequence of discrete actions \parencite{Saldana2015}. Each process code was matched with an in vivo code drawn directly from the transcript, preserving a direct connection to the underlying data for subsequent analytic passes \parencite{Saldana2015}. Following the first cycle, a code mapping pass consolidated the resulting raw codes, correcting typographical and minor wording variations without altering the underlying coding decisions. A second coding cycle then applied pattern coding \parencite{Saldana2015}, combining first-cycle codes of related meaning into a smaller set of pattern codes and reducing the overall code space to a size suitable for further thematic analysis; the specific procedure applied during this cycle is described in Section~\ref{ssec:coding-results}.

The resulting pattern codes were combined into second-order themes and aggregate dimensions, as outlined above. These aggregate dimensions and their constituent themes were then interpreted through the lens of the meta-requirements and design-requirements established in Section~\ref{ssec:meta-requirements}, from which the initial design principles are derived. This connects the inductively constructed data structure back to the pre-existing theoretical framework, mirroring Gioia's own emphasis on relating the resulting data structure to existing theory when constructing the grounded theory model \parencite{Gioia2013,Gioia2021}. Because codes are counted at each stage of the analysis, this process additionally permits a limited degree of quantitative analysis of the empirical findings, complementing the qualitative construction of themes and dimensions.

The resulting initial design principles are evaluated through step~\rom{7} of the method for design principle development (cf. Section~\ref{ssec:mdpd}), applying semi-structured interviews with practitioners and academics in \ac{HCI} and \ac{XAI}. % TODO: confirm exact criteria wording against Iivari2021 / Janiesch2020 before finalising
Each design principle is evaluated against the criteria proposed by \textcite{Iivari2021} and applied by \textcite{Janiesch2020}. Feedback gathered during the interviews is used to refine, and where necessary revise, the design principles. The full interview guide, including the general question set and the design-principle-specific probes, is provided in Appendix~\ref{apx:interview-guide}.
