How well an explanation achieves understanding for a given user can be measured through explanation satisfaction and is distinct from the goodness of the explanation itself \parencite{Hoffman2023}. This aligns with DP2 by \textcite{Weisz2025}, which calls for tailoring outputs to the user's knowledge and capabilities. While DR1 affects the cognitive load of the user, relating it to MR3, it primarily addresses the user's attribution of \ac{ToM} to the system (MR1).
%     \item \textbf{Explain local behaviour.} Extending G11 by \textcite{Amershi2019} to individual outputs, explanations should explain the system's local behaviour. \textcite{Weisz2025} emphasise the impact of generative variability and how this particularly affects the understanding of local behaviour. Explaining local behaviour derives from MR1, by reducing the risk of misattribution of \ac{ToM} to the system.
%     \item \textbf{Account for unfaithful explanations.} Explanations such as \ac{CoT} are not guaranteed to reflect the system's underlying reasoning \parencite{Lyu2023}, corrupting the user's mental model and leading to miscalibrated reliance. This violates MR1 and MR2, and is therefore a critical design consideration. A related concern appears in DP3 by \textcite{Weisz2025}, though their notion of faithfulness is grounded in source material rather than correspondence to the system's reasoning. The goal is to design for the possibility of unfaithful explanations, not to guarantee faithfulness.
%     \item \textbf{Time explanation delivery.} The timing of explanation delivery should be oriented towards the user's curiosity and need for explanation \parencite[cf. G3,][]{Amershi2019}. Explanations should be provided when the user encounters unexpected behaviour or when their mental model is challenged, rather than preemptively or indiscriminately \parencite{Miller2019,Hoffman2023}. Supplying explanations too early may anchor the user's considerations \parencite{Jussupow2021}. This DR addresses MR3 by supporting appropriate cognitive engagement, but also MR1, because an explanation delivered too early undermines the user's ability to form an independent assessment of the system's behaviour.
%     \item \textbf{Guard against passive acceptance.} Addressing MR2 and MR3, by supporting appropriate cognitive engagement, explanations should be designed to avoid heuristic acceptance of the system's outputs, because mere presentation of an explanation does not guarantee appropriate processing, as demonstrated by \textcite{Jussupow2021}. Explanations, combined with additional interventions such as friction, should be designed to support deliberate processing where appropriate \parencite{Buinca2021,Weisz2025}.
%     \item \textbf{Constrain post-hoc dismissal.} An explanation can provide users with material to argue for the dismissal of system outputs, even when that dismissal is unwarranted, like the \enquote{explaining away} pattern in \textcite{Jussupow2021}. As the user is already deliberately processing the system's outputs (unlike DR5), inducing engagement does not address this risk. To address the violations of MR2 and MR3, explanations should be embedded in an interaction design that discourages unfounded dismissal of system outputs.
%     \item \textbf{Select explanation content.} The content of an explanation should present a subset of sufficient causes rather than a complete causal chain \parencite{Miller2019}. An exhaustive causal account increases the mental load of the user without necessarily improving their understanding. Measures of abnormality and intentionality can guide the selection of causes \parencite{Hilton2017}. This directly addresses MR3, by avoiding unnecessary cognitive load.
