\section{Introduction} \label{sec:introduction}

The emergence of \ac{GenAI} has created unprecedented interest in the field of \ac{AI}. This public interest has fuelled a surge in research and development, leading to the rapid advancement of \ac{GenAI} technologies. Particularly, advancements in \ac{LLM} have enabled the creation and widespread adoption of chatbot such as ChatGPT \parencite{OpenAI2025a} or specialised tools like Claude Code \parencite{AnthropicInc2026}. Due to the rapid development of technology, research in the fields of \ac{HAII} and \ac{XAI} fallen behind \parencite{Adadi2018}.

Mental models play a significant role in shaping human interaction with \ac{AI} systems. They influence how users perceive these systems, how they interpret their outputs, and how they rely on \ac{AI} \parencite{Miller2019}. Mental models are subject to change over time, particularly as users gain experience with \ac{AI} systems \parencite{Schneider2025}. Anthropomorphism also plays an important role in the formation of mental models: users may attribute human-like qualities to \ac{AI} systems \parencite{Bodamer2026}.

Explainability is crucial for building trust with and acceptance of \ac{AI} systems \parencite{Arrieta2020}. Established methods such as \acs{LIME} \parencite{Ribeiro2016} and \acs{SHAP} \parencite{Lundberg2017} have been developed and validated to provide explanations for discriminative models. These methods however, are not directly applicable to \ac{GenAI} systems, for which no established methods exist. In addition, \textcite{Bienefeld2023} have shown that, even for discriminative models, many explanations methods are too technical to be effective for lay users.

The growing adoption of \ac{GenAI} in various domains, including healthcare and education, highlights the need for measures that enable effective and responsible use of these systems. Recent research has shown, that mental models have impact on task performance in human-AI collaboration settings \parencite{Jussupow2021, Vaccaro2024}. Only if users have an accurate mental model of the \ac{AI} system, they can effectively collaborate with it. Due to the nascent state of the field, two key areas remain underexplored \parencite{Bodamer2026,Weisz2025}:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Understanding how users form and update their mental models of explainable \ac{LLM}-based systems, especially in the context of collaborative problem-solving tasks.
    \item Limited research has been conducted on the explainability of \ac{LLM}-based systems, leading to a lack of design guidelines for supporting accurate mental models in human-\ac{LLM} collaboration, for such systems.
\end{enumerate}

This thesis seeks to address these gaps by analysing the transcripts of a user study, in which participants collaborated with an explainable \ac{LLM}-based system to solve maths problems. From the analysis, design principles for explainable \ac{LLM}-based systems are derived. The rest of this thesis is structured as follows: The fundamental concepts and related works from the fields of \ac{XAI}, \ac{GenAI} and cognitive psychology are explained in Chapter~\ref{sec:fundamentals}. The research questions are presented at the end of this chapter. Chapter~\ref{sec:methodology} explains \ac{DSR} as research methodology and describes the specific research methods employed in this thesis. Chapter~\ref{sec:problem-awareness} presents a literature review on current challenges in the field of \ac{HAII}, highlighting current research on explanations for \ac{AI} systems and their impact on users' mental models. The user study evaluated in this thesis is described in Chapter~\ref{sec:artefact-design}, with particular focus on the chatbots created for the study. Chapter~\ref{sec:design-principles} carries the majority of the analytic work, discussing the analysis procedure, the derivation process for the design principles, and the evaluation of the design principles. Chapter~\ref{sec:discussion} synthesises the findings, discusses their implications in the broader research context. The chapter concludes outlining the limitations of the thesis and providing directions for future research. Finally, Chapter~\ref{sec:conclusion} closes the thesis with a summary of the findings.
